# Missing You — A Plain-Language Guide for Everyone

*You don’t need a technical background to read this. If you’ve ever wished you had a quiet, respectful place to remember someone—and wondered why “the internet” doesn’t always feel trustworthy for something this personal—this guide is for you.*

---

## 1. Introduction: What Is Missing You?

**Missing You** is a gentle online journal for memories of people you love and miss. You sign in with email, write in a calm, uncluttered space, and choose what stays private and what you might share later—always on your terms.

**Who it’s for**

- Anyone who wants a dedicated place to remember someone, separate from social feeds and group chats.
- People who value **privacy** for raw, honest writing—and still want **clarity** about what is real and unchanged when they do choose to share.
- Families and friends who may read a shared memory someday and deserve to know it hasn’t been silently edited or “re-written” after the fact.

**What human need it addresses**

Grief and remembrance don’t fit neatly into likes, comments, or disappearing stories. Many of us want something slower: a private desk drawer for words we’re not ready to show anyone, and—when we’re ready—a way to share that feels **dignified** and **trustworthy**. Missing You is built around that rhythm: write first, protect your words, then decide if and how they travel.

**Why someone might use it**

- To capture stories, details, and feelings before they fade—without performing them for an audience.
- To have one organized place for memories (and, if you want, a path to share a single page with people you trust).
- To optionally add a **time-stamped, independently checkable record** that a particular memory existed as you saved it—not to “invest” or “trade,” but to anchor meaning in a world where digital things are easy to change and erase.

---

## 2. Why This Product Exists

**The gap**

Most everyday apps are built for speed, engagement, or advertising—not for the slow work of memory. A note on your phone can be lost, duplicated, or edited without a trace. Social platforms mix your grief with everything else, and their rules, layouts, and priorities change without asking you.

**Why a normal website isn’t always “enough”**

A traditional website can absolutely offer a beautiful journal. What it usually *can’t* offer—by itself—is a **public, neutral place** that keeps a simple promise: *“This fingerprint of your memory was recorded at this time, and anyone can check that the record still matches.”* That kind of check doesn’t live in one company’s database. It lives on a **shared network** designed to make certain records hard to quietly rewrite.

**What makes this experience different**

Missing You tries to hold two things at once:

- **A warm, human experience**—bilingual support (English and Traditional Chinese), thoughtful design, and privacy by default.
- **An honest technical choice**—your **full writing stays in secure, private storage** you access through the app; only a **small, intentional “proof”** can be placed on a public network if *you* choose to anchor it.

The product isn’t trying to replace therapy, community, or paper journals. It’s trying to give remembrance a **purpose-built home**—and, when you want it, a **verifiable thread** between your words and a record that doesn’t depend on a single company’s say-so.

## 3. What Is Blockchain? (For Complete Beginners)

Think of a **blockchain** as a **shared digital logbook** many computers help maintain together. When something is written into that logbook according to the rules, it becomes **very difficult to change later without everyone noticing**. People sometimes call it a **public ledger**: not “public” in the sense of exposing your secrets, but “public” in the sense that **the rules and the entries many people care about are visible and checkable**.

**A simple analogy**

Imagine a town square bulletin board where, once a notice is posted in the official book, the townspeople keep copies. If someone tried to tear out a page or alter a past notice, the copies wouldn’t match—and the mismatch would be obvious. Blockchains use math and networked agreement instead of paper, but the *idea* is similar: **certain entries gain permanence and transparency**, not secrecy.

**What people often mean by “permanence”**

In everyday life, a file can be edited and saved over the old version. On a blockchain, the point is often the opposite for **specific records**: once written, altering them is designed to be **impractical or obvious**. That doesn’t make everything “forever” in a spiritual sense—but it changes the **digital** story from “easy to rewrite quietly” to “costly or visible to tamper with.”

**What blockchain is NOT (in the way we use it here)**

- **Not** a slot machine for money.
- **Not** a requirement to buy coins or follow market news.
- **Not** a place where your private journal paragraphs are posted for the world to read.

In Missing You, blockchain is **optional infrastructure for trust**—like a notary’s ledger, not like a billboard for your diary.

---

## 4. Why Does Missing You Use Blockchain?

This is the heart of the matter.

**What actually gets recorded on-chain**

When you choose to **anchor** a memory, Missing You does *not* put your story on the blockchain. It puts a **digital fingerprint** (a kind of unique summary) and **basic facts** the system needs for verification—things like identifiers and timing—so that later, anyone allowed to see your shared page can confirm: **this text still matches what was anchored**.

It’s the difference between engraving your whole letter in marble in the town square—which would be exposing and irreversible—and placing a **sealed, numbered reference** in a public record that says: **“Entry #47 existed in this form at this time.”** Your letter stays with you; the reference is what’s public.

**Why not “just use a normal database”?**

A company database is excellent for speed, search, and privacy controls—and Missing You **uses one** for your full writing, because that’s the right place for the human, sensitive part of the product.

A database alone, though, is **controlled by whoever runs it**. If someone asked, “Prove this record hasn’t been altered since 2023,” the honest answer is often: **“Trust us.”** Blockchain, used carefully, adds an answer that sounds different: **“Don’t only trust us—check the shared record.”**

**What you gain, in real life**

- **Clarity in fragile moments** — When memory is shared after loss, doubt can be painful. Verification doesn’t replace love or truth—but it can reduce **needless suspicion** about whether a digital page was silently changed.
- **A sense of stewardship** — Some people want their words to have a **lineage**: not uploaded into a black box, but connected to something **checkable** when it matters.
- **Separation of roles** — Your intimate prose stays protected in the app’s private storage; the chain holds **only what’s needed for integrity**, by design.

**Emotional honesty**

Blockchain doesn’t fix grief. It doesn’t make a company perfect. What it *can* do—when used minimally and on purpose—is support a **specific promise** about **integrity and timing** for the memories you choose to anchor. Missing You tries to use it **only** for that promise, and not as decoration or hype.

## 5. Do I Need to Understand Crypto to Use This?

**No.**

You can use Missing You as a **journal first**: sign in with email, write, save, and keep entries private. The interface is built around **people and memories**, not around wallets and jargon.

**When blockchain shows up**

If you decide to anchor a memory, the product may ask you to connect a **digital wallet**—think of it as a **keychain** that lets you approve a specific action on the shared network. You still don’t need to “be into crypto.” You need to be able to follow clear steps, the way you’d confirm a payment or approve a secure login.

**Behind the scenes**

Most of the complexity—hashes, networks, verification—is handled so you can focus on **writing** and **choices** (private, shared, anchored or not). The technology is there to **support** the experience, not to turn you into a trader or an engineer.

## 6. Privacy, Trust & Safety

**What you control**

- **Your words** — Meant to stay private in the app’s systems unless you choose to share.
- **Visibility** — Private versus shareable is yours to decide, within how the product works today.
- **Anchoring** — Optional. If you never anchor, your experience can remain that of a careful, private journal.

**What the blockchain does and doesn’t expose**

- The chain does **not** need your full essay to do its job in Missing You. It holds **proof-oriented information**, not your intimate paragraphs.
- Anything **public on-chain** is, by nature, **visible to someone who knows how to look**. That’s why the product is designed to keep **sensitive prose out** of that layer.

**Emotional trust**

Technology can’t guarantee how anyone will feel. What Missing You aims for is **straightforwardness**: clear defaults, clear limits, and a design that respects that remembrance deserves **dignity**—not dark patterns, not surprise exposure, and not treating your grief as content for a feed.

**If you’re skeptical**

That’s reasonable. New tools deserve scrutiny, especially around loss. Ask questions, read the shorter [Product Guide for Users](./user-readme.md), and use only what feels right for you—including **skipping** blockchain entirely if anchoring doesn’t serve your needs.

## 7. The Big Vision

Missing You is trying to help build a future where **digital remembrance** feels as intentional as lighting a candle—not as frantic as scrolling, and not as flimsy as a file that could vanish with the next app redesign.

The vision is **human-centered**: technology in the **background**, clarity in the **foreground**, and honesty about **what a product can and can’t do** for the heart.

If blockchain belongs anywhere in ordinary life, it might be here: not to shout, not to speculate—but to offer a **quiet, verifiable witness** to the words we choose to stand by, when we’re ready.
