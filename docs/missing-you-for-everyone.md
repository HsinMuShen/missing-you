# Missing You — A Plain-Language Guide for Everyone

**Live site:** [English](https://missing-you.hsinmushen.com/en) · [繁體中文](https://missing-you.hsinmushen.com/zh-TW)

*You don’t need a technical background to read this. If you’ve ever wanted a quiet, respectful place to remember someone—and felt unsure about handing something so personal to “something on the internet”—this guide is for you.*

---

## What Is Missing You?

**Missing You** is an online space for remembrance.

You can write about someone you miss, moments that stay with you, or feelings you are not ready to put in front of the world. You sign in with email and write in a simple, calm interface. What stays only for you, and what you might share later, is yours to decide at every step. The product is not built to push you toward public sharing; it is built to respect your choice to wait.

**Who it’s for**

- Anyone who wants a dedicated place for someone—not memories scattered across stories, feeds, or group chats.
- People who care about **privacy** for honest writing, and who also want **clarity** that, if they ever share, what others read is what they truly wrote.
- Families and friends who may read a shared page someday and deserve confidence that the words were not quietly rewritten.

**The slower need Missing You answers**

Grief, longing, and memory do not fit likes, comment threads, or posts that vanish after a day. Some people need a slower rhythm: a letter slid into a drawer, not shown to anyone until they are ready—then shared with dignity and clarity. Missing You tries to create that rhythm: a private place to write, and your own decision about whether it ever needs to be seen.

**How Missing You can walk beside you**

You can capture feelings and details while they are still vivid, without performing for an audience. You can keep memories in one place. If you choose to share later, you do not have to lay your whole life open—only the page you pick, for people you trust.

If you want to go further, Missing You also offers an optional path: using blockchain to leave a **time-stamped, checkable** record of a memory. Here, blockchain is not for investing or trading; it is one way, in a digital world, to give something important a harder-to-erase trace.

---

## Why This Product Exists

**Some important things everyday tools are not built for**

Most apps are built to be fast, always-on, and interactive. They are good at sharing and exposure. Remembering a person often runs the other way.

Notes on a phone can be overwritten or lost across devices. Social platforms drop feelings into a huge stream where algorithms, layouts, and rules change—and your memories move with them. Missing You exists to hold space for needs those tools often overlook.

**Isn’t a normal website enough?**

A regular site can be a beautiful journal. Usually, though, trust still rests on one company: you hope the text is safe and unchanged, but that hope is “trust us,” not something outsiders can verify.

If what you care about is not only “stored somewhere,” but **showing that a memory existed in a given form at a given time—and can still be checked**—you need more than storage alone. You need a way to check that does not depend only on one operator’s word.

**Two things Missing You tries to hold at once**

**Human feeling** — tone, layout, and pace are intentionally calm. Entries start private by default.

**Technical honesty** — your full text stays in secure, private storage. Only if you choose it does the system add a small verifiable proof of that memory’s existence at a point in time.

It does not try to replace therapy, community, or a paper diary. It is a place reserved for missing someone—and, when you need it, a clearer, more trustworthy way to record what mattered.

## What Is Blockchain?

Blockchain can sound intimidating; the core idea is simple. Picture a **shared digital ledger** many computers keep in step. Once something is written by the rules, changing old entries without others noticing becomes very hard—because copies exist and mismatches show up.

It is closer to a **publicly checkable record** than to putting your secrets on a billboard. For Missing You, the chain is not for displaying or storing your full story. If you opt in, it can hold proof that a memory **existed in a given form at a given time**—not your private paragraphs.

---

## Why Does Missing You Use Blockchain?

When you **anchor** a memory, Missing You does **not** put your full text on-chain. What is recorded is a designed **digital fingerprint** and the verification metadata needed so that, later, people you allow to see a shared page can confirm the text still matches—rather than having been silently altered. Your writing stays in your hands; what is public on-chain is a reference others can check.

That can bring a **quiet** kind of assurance. In fragile moments you may not need the whole world to understand—but you may still want important words to have a clear **lineage**. Blockchain does not heal loss or answer grief. On **integrity**—whether a digital page matches what was committed—it can add support so what mattered is less easy to quietly rewrite in time.

---

## Do I Need to Understand Crypto?

**No.**

You can use Missing You as a simple, private digital journal: sign in, write, save. That is the heart of the experience.

Only if you **choose** to anchor might you need a digital wallet—and even then it acts more like an **approval tool** than an invitation to “crypto culture.” Technology appears when it serves you; your focus stays on your words and what you want to hold.

---

## Privacy, Safety, and Trust

Missing You starts from one premise: your words belong to you first. Content is private by default; sharing is your choice; anchoring is always optional. Sensitive writing stays in private storage, not spread across the public web. If you use the chain, only the **minimum** verification information is placed there.

For Missing You, technology should never sit above human feeling.

---

## The Future Missing You Wants to Help Build

Missing You is not only another product shape—it is an invitation to a **quieter** kind of mental room.

In that room, longing does not need to be measured in traffic or forced into the open. People and memories that matter are not pushed by algorithms or asked to prove their weight in a flood of information. They can be lit like a small candle: placed with care, kept safe, and passed on when the time is right.

Technology is still there, but not at the front of the stage. What matters are people, memory, and feelings that were real and deserve gentleness. Blockchain here is not a slogan, noise, or speculation. When you need it, it is only a way for some important lines to carry a trace that is harder for time and neglect to quietly erase.

Some people leave before every word is said. That does not mean everything has to blur away.

Missing You wants to leave a simple, quiet possibility: memory not only **stored**, but **cherished**—missing not only felt, but given a place where it can rest.
